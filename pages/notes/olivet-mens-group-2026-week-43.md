    21. For as by a man came death, by a man has come also the resurrection of the dead.

    30. Why are we in danger every hour?

    32. What do I gain if, humanly speaking, I fought with beasts at Ephesus? If the dead are not raised, “Let us eat and drink, for tomorrow we die.”
    33. Do not be deceived: “Bad company ruins good morals.”
    34. Wake up from your drunken stupor, as is right, and do not go on sinning. For some have no knowledge of God. I say this to your shame.
    35. But someone will ask, “How are the dead raised? With what kind of body do they come?”

    57. But thanks be to God, who gives us the victory through our Lord Jesus Christ.
    58. Therefore, my beloved brothers, be steadfast, immovable, always abounding in the work of the Lord, knowing that in the Lord your labor is not in vain.

    8. For we do not want you to be unaware, brothers, of the affliction we experienced in Asia. For we were so utterly burdened beyond our strength that we despaired of life itself.
    9. Indeed, we felt that we had received the sentence of death. But that was to make us rely not on ourselves but on God who raises the dead.
    10. He delivered us from such a deadly peril, and he will deliver us. On him we have set our hope that he will deliver us again.
    11. You also must help us by prayer, so that many will give thanks on our behalf for the blessing granted us through the prayers of many.

## Day 3 — 2 Corinthians 3-4

??? scripture "2 Corinthians 3"

    1. Are we beginning to commend ourselves again? Or do we need, as some do, letters of recommendation to you, or from you?
    2. You yourselves are our letter of recommendation, written on our hearts, to be known and read by all.
    3. And you show that you are a letter from Christ delivered by us, written not with ink but with the Spirit of the living God, not on tablets of stone but on tablets of human hearts.
    4. Such is the confidence that we have through Christ toward God.
    5. Not that we are sufficient in ourselves to claim anything as coming from us, but our sufficiency is from God,
    6. who has made us sufficient to be ministers of a new covenant, not of the letter but of the Spirit. For the letter kills, but the Spirit gives life.
    7. Now if the ministry of death, carved in letters on stone, came with such glory that the Israelites could not gaze at Moses’ face because of its glory, which was being brought to an end,
    8. will not the ministry of the Spirit have even more glory?
    9. For if there was glory in the ministry of condemnation, the ministry of righteousness must far exceed it in glory.
    10. Indeed, in this case, what once had glory has come to have no glory at all, because of the glory that surpasses it.
    11. For if what was being brought to an end came with glory, much more will what is permanent have glory.
    12. Since we have such a hope, we are very bold,
    13. not like Moses, who would put a veil over his face so that the Israelites might not gaze at the outcome of what was being brought to an end.
    14. But their minds were hardened. For to this day, when they read the old covenant, that same veil remains unlifted, because only through Christ is it taken away.
    15. Yes, to this day whenever Moses is read a veil lies over their hearts.
    16. But when one turns to the Lord, the veil is removed.
    17. Now the Lord is the Spirit, and where the Spirit of the Lord is, there is freedom.
    18. And we all, with unveiled face, beholding the glory of the Lord, are being transformed into the same image from one degree of glory to another. For this comes from the Lord who is the Spirit.

    1. Therefore, having this ministry by the mercy of God, we do not lose heart.
    2. But we have renounced disgraceful, underhanded ways. We refuse to practice cunning or to tamper with God’s word, but by the open statement of the truth we would commend ourselves to everyone’s conscience in the sight of God.
    3. And even if our gospel is veiled, it is veiled to those who are perishing.

    5. For what we proclaim is not ourselves, but Jesus Christ as Lord, with ourselves as your servants for Jesus’ sake.
    6. For God, who said, “Let light shine out of darkness,” has shone in our hearts to give the light of the knowledge of the glory of God in the face of Jesus Christ.
    7. But we have this treasure in jars of clay, to show that the surpassing power belongs to God and not to us.
    8. We are afflicted in every way, but not crushed; perplexed, but not driven to despair;
    9. persecuted, but not forsaken; struck down, but not destroyed;
    10. always carrying in the body the death of Jesus, so that the life of Jesus also may be manifested in our bodies.
    11. For we who live are always being given over to death for Jesus’ sake, so that the life of Jesus also may be manifested in our mortal flesh.
    12. So death is at work in us, but life in you.
    13. Since we have the same spirit of faith according to what has been written, “I believed, and so I spoke,” we also believe, and so we also speak,
    14. knowing that he who raised the Lord Jesus will raise us also with Jesus and bring us with you into his presence.
    15. For it is all for your sake, so that as grace extends to more and more people it may increase thanksgiving, to the glory of God.
    16. So we do not lose heart. Though our outer self is wasting away, our inner self is being renewed day by day.
    17. For this light momentary affliction is preparing for us an eternal weight of glory beyond all comparison,
    18. as we look not to the things that are seen but to the things that are unseen. For the things that are seen are transient, but the things that are unseen are eternal.

## Historical Context — 1 Corinthians 15-16 · 2 Corinthians 1-8

1 Corinthians was written from Ephesus (~AD 55); chapters 15-16 are its climax — the longest sustained resurrection argument in the New Testament, followed by travel plans and the Jerusalem collection. Between the two canonical letters, the relationship nearly broke: Timothy's visit failed, a "painful visit" ended with Paul publicly insulted, and a tearful "severe letter" (now lost) went to Corinth with Titus. 2 Corinthians was written from Macedonia (~AD 56) after Titus finally returned with good news — relief, vindication, and unfinished collection business all at once.

**Day 1 · 1 Corinthians 15-16 — the resurrection chapter and the collection**

- Verses 3-5 are a pre-Pauline creed — "I delivered to you what I also received" is technical tradition language. Most scholars date it to within ~5 years of the crucifixion (many say ~3, at Paul's Jerusalem visit) — some of the oldest recoverable Christian material in the New Testament.
- The appearance to "more than five hundred brothers at one time" is unique to Paul, and "most are still alive" reads like an invitation to fact-check him ~25 years after the events.
- "Baptized on behalf of the dead" (v29) has generated dozens of scholarly interpretations — Gordon Fee catalogues ~40, other counts run past 200. Vicarious baptism for dead converts and baptism motivated by dead loved ones are the main families. Paul's argument is logical (why bother if there's no resurrection?), not an endorsement of the practice.
- The Adam/Christ typology (v21-22, 45-49) reads Genesis 1-2 through the resurrection: two humanities, two heads, two destinies.
- The collection instructions (16:1-4) — weekly, proportional, first-day set-aside — are among the earliest evidence for a Sunday rhythm in the church. "The saints" means the Jerusalem church, famine-poor since Acts 11.
- Aquila and Prisca host a church in their Ephesus house (16:19) — the same couple expelled from Rome under Claudius (~AD 49, the Orosius date), running a congregation wherever they landed.

**Day 2 · 2 Corinthians 1-2 — the crisis behind the letter**

- Paul's changed travel plan (1:15-17) was weaponized as proof of fickleness — "does he make plans according to the flesh?" The real reason for the detour was pastoral: avoiding another "painful visit" (2:1).
- The painful visit (~AD 55): an emergency trip from Ephesus where someone publicly wronged Paul and the church failed to defend him (2:5-8; 7:12). The offender was "punished by the majority" — evidence the church could vote on discipline.
- The severe letter (2:3-4) — written "out of much affliction and anguish of heart and with many tears" — is lost, unless chapters 10-13 preserve it. Paul gambled on confrontation he couldn't supervise.
- "Triumphal procession" (2:14) is the Roman triumph: a general parading captives through the city with incense — celebration to the victors, death-smell to the condemned. Paul places himself among the captives, Christ's prisoner on parade.
- Abandoning an "open door" at Troas because Titus wasn't there (2:12-13) shows how much was riding on one report from Corinth.

**Day 3 · 2 Corinthians 3-4 — new covenant ministry**

- "Letters of recommendation" (3:1) were ancient LinkedIn endorsements — itinerant teachers carried credential letters between churches. Paul's answer: the Corinthians themselves are his letter, written by the Spirit on hearts (echoing Ezekiel 36:26 and Jeremiah 31:33).
- Moses' veil (3:12-16) is Paul's midrash on Exodus 34:29-35 — the veil that hid fading glory becomes an image of minds that can't read Scripture until they "turn to the Lord."
- "The letter kills, but the Spirit gives life" (3:6) is not law-bad/gospel-good: it's stone tablets vs heart tablets — same covenant God, different medium.
- "Jars of clay" (4:7) were the cheapest pottery — the disposable tupperware of antiquity. Some hear Gideon's torches hidden in jars (Judges 7) behind the image. Either way: treasure deliberately housed in the breakable.
- "Light momentary affliction" (4:17) is ironic from a man who just said he was "utterly burdened beyond our strength" (1:8) — the comparison is about weight class, not dismissal.

**Day 4 · 2 Corinthians 5-6 — ambassadors of reconciliation**

- The tent vs building contrast (5:1-5) reuses tabernacle/temple language for the body — temporary dwelling vs permanent architecture.
- The "judgment seat" (5:10) is the bema — the raised tribunal every Roman city had. Paul had stood before Corinth's own bema when Gallio heard his case (Acts 18:12-17); readers could picture the architecture.
- "Ambassadors" (5:20) is imperial vocabulary — the presbeutēs delivered terms, he didn't negotiate them. Paul's embassy language frames the gospel as a decree from a higher court than Rome's.
- "Unequally yoked" (6:14) borrows Deuteronomy 22:10's ban on plowing with a mixed yoke — applied here to partnerships entangled with idolatrous institutions. In Corinth that meant guilds, courts, and temple commerce, not marriage advice.
- The quotation chain in 6:16-18 fuses Leviticus 26:11-12 (God dwelling among his people), Isaiah 52:11 (exodus separation), and 2 Samuel 7:14 (the sonship promise to David) — temple, exodus, and covenant in three lines.

**Day 5 · 2 Corinthians 7-8 — grief that worked, and the poor who begged to give**

- "Godly grief produces repentance... worldly grief produces death" (7:10) — the severe letter's gamble paid off. Paul even admits regretting it briefly (7:8) before Titus' report changed everything.
- The Macedonian paradox (8:1-5): "extreme poverty" that "overflowed in a wealth of generosity" — they begged for the charis (favor/grace) of participating. Philippi, Thessalonica, and Berea were poorer than Corinth and gave more.
- The collection was promised in Galatians 2:10 ("remember the poor" — the Jerusalem pillars' one condition) and served double duty: famine relief and Jew-Gentile unity, Gentile churches carrying a gift to the mother church.
- "Equality" (8:13-15) is isotēs — and Paul quotes the manna economy of Exodus 16:18: whoever gathered much had nothing left over.
- The delegation (8:16-24): Titus plus "the brother who is famous among all the churches" plus a tested third — a three-man custody chain so no one could accuse Paul of skimming. Acts 20:4 names a seven-man delegation when the money finally travels.
- "Though he was rich, yet for your sake he became poor" (8:9) — the gospel stated in financial grammar, aimed at a church slow to give.

## HEAR Journal

2 cor 4:6. For God, who said, “Let light shine out of darkness,” has shone in our hearts to give the light of the knowledge of the glory of God in the face of Jesus Christ.
2 corinthians 4:16. So we do not lose heart. Though our outer self is wasting away, our inner self is being renewed day by day.
2 corinthians 4:17. For this light momentary affliction is preparing for us an eternal weight of glory beyond all comparison,
2 corinthians 4:18. as we look not to the things that are seen but to the things that are unseen. For the things that are seen are transient, but the things that are unseen are eternal.

### Explain

Paul is writing this letter to defend his apostolic ministry, to argue/woo the Corinthians, who have been led astray by the "super-apostles" - back to obedience to Jesus through reconciliation with Paul himself. This section of the letter, chapter 4, includes the inclusio of "do not lose heart" emphasizing a point Paul is making in this text that believers have the knowledge of God (vv 6) and because of that knowledge we **know** that the world fades away and eternity is coming, affliction today will not even be comparable to the glory awaiting us with the Lord.

### Apply

Reflecting on my year, I wouldn't say I've been "led astray" like the Corinthians, but I certainly have given myself too much to work and it's been taxing and fatiguing. I don't believe that I'm in rebellion like the Corinthians, I don't need Paul to show up with jealousy as if I'm being led astray by a false gospel - but I have been tired (for years) and I need the encouragement that the outer self is passing away but the inner self is being renewed day by day (vv 16) because that is the reality, it's a reality I have knowledge of (vv 6) and through that knowledge the light of Christ can and should shine in my heart and that in and of itself ought to help me press on. Furthermore, the conviction through the encouragement is to continue to make some steps in erecting boundaries with work while preserving my good reputation (a challenge for another day)

### Respond

Lord thank you for the blessing of the knowledge of Christ that ought to, and does, lift up my heart when I am weary. May it never be that Paul would need to write this letter to me or to Olivet, and thank you for the encouragement in this text and the larger context of 2 Corinthians through which your people (such as Olivet) can be encouraged through the lives and ministry of saints before us.
