## Corinth archaeology

### Gallio's proconsulship (Acts 18:12-17) — used wk 42
Verdict: confirmed — Delphi inscription (Claudius' letter to Delphi) names Gallio
proconsul of Achaia; dates his term to ~AD 51-52. Standard anchor for all Pauline
chronology.
Approved wording: "an inscription at Delphi fixes Gallio's term to AD 51–52 —
the anchor of Pauline chronology"
Sources: Metzger Collection MC 0463 · bibleodyssey.org/articles/erastus-gallio-and-paul/ · Koch, Münster (uni-muenster.de)

### Diolkos — used wk 42
Verdict: real, excavated (Verdelis 1956-62) — but Pettegrew (AJA 2011) shows it
mainly served warships and Corinth's own traffic, not mass transshipment.
Approved wording: "shipping and exchange flowed through (how much passed over
the diolkos itself is debated)"
Sources: Werner, Int'l J. Nautical Archaeology 26 (1997) · Pettegrew, AJA 115.4 (2011) · korinthos.gr/en/diolkos/

### Macellum meat market (1 Cor 10:25) — used wk 42
Verdict: confirmed — Latin MACELLVM inscription found 1898 (Cornelius Secundus
dedication, Augustan), fragmentary. Gk μάκελλον is a Latin loanword.
Approved wording: "excavated, with fragments of its dedicatory inscription
surviving"
Sources: Corinth Computer Project (corinth.sas.upenn.edu) · Gill, Tyndale Bull. 43.2 (1992) · Kent, Corinth VIII.3

### "Synagogue of the Hebrews" lintel — used wk 42
Verdict: real but late — limestone lintel found 1898 on the Lechaion Road reads
[SYNA]GOGE EBR[AION]; dated 4th-5th c. CE — centuries after Paul. Crude lettering;
provenance questioned by some.
Approved wording: "a lintel inscribed 'Synagogue of the Hebrews' — dated
centuries later (4th-5th c.), but evidence the community outlasted him"
Sources: Jewish Museum of Greece / CJA · holylandphotos.org GSPLCO02 · Kent Corinth VIII.3

### Isthmian Games wreath (1 Cor 9:25) — used wk 42
Verdict: confirmed — held ~10 mi east every 2 years, second only to Olympics;
prize wreath was pine (post-destruction era), earlier wild celery (selinon).
Approved wording: "a wreath of pine or celery — perishable by design"
Sources: Hastings DNT "Prize" · TIPs translation commentary on 1 Cor 9:25

### Corinth refounded — used wk 42
Verdict: standard — Roman colony refounded by Julius Caesar in 44 BC; twin ports
Lechaion (west, Italy) and Cenchreae (east, Aegean/Asia).
Sources: general consensus (Strabo 8.6.23; Plutarch Caes. 57)

### Erastus inscription (Rom 16:23) — used wk 42
Verdict: inscription confirmed, identification debated — 1929, NE of theater:
"Erastus, in return for his aedileship, laid this pavement at his own expense."
Aedile vs. city "oikonomos" mismatch and name commonness keep the ID contested.
Approved wording: "an inscription records an Erastus paving near the theater —
possibly the man of Romans 16:23, debated"
Sources: bibleodyssey.org/articles/erastus-gallio-and-paul/ · Gill, Tyndale Bull.; Corinth VIII.3 #232

## Pauline chronology & biography

### Claudius' expulsion of Jews (Acts 18:2) — used wk 43, 44
Verdict: event confirmed, date contested — Suetonius Claud. 25.4: Jews "constantly
rioting at the instigation of Chrestus" expelled. Orosius gives yr 9 (~AD 49);
Cassius Dio 60.6.6 suggests ~AD 41 (and says restriction, not expulsion).
Approved wording: "expelled under Claudius (~AD 49, the Orosius date)"
Sources: Suetonius Claud. 25.4 · Orosius Hist. 7.6.15-16 · Cassius Dio 60.6.6 · Wikipedia "Claudius' expulsion"

### Aquila & Prisca — used wk 43
Verdict: house church attested in Ephesus (1 Cor 16:19) and Rome (Rom 16:3-5);
hosting a church in Corinth is not explicit. Don't say "third house church."
Approved wording: "a congregation wherever they landed"
Sources: text-internal (Acts 18; Rom 16:3-5; 1 Cor 16:19)

### Aretas IV & Damascus (2 Cor 11:32) — used wk 44
Verdict: association supported — reign 9 BC–AD 40; gap in Damascene Roman coins
AD 34-62 permits a Nabataean window. Campbell (JBL 2002) argues precisely AD 36-37.
Exact year is argument, not certainty.
Approved wording: "Aretas IV's ethnarch at Damascus — likely AD 36-37 (Campbell),
though the exact year is argued from a coinage gap, not certain"
Sources: Campbell, JBL 121 (2002) · Cambridge Greek Testament on 2 Cor 11

### Corinthian letter sequence — used wk 42, 43
Verdict: standard reconstruction, text-internal — previous letter lost (1 Cor 5:9),
1 Corinthians, failed Timothy visit, painful visit, severe/tearful letter lost
(2 Cor 2:3-4; 7:8), then 2 Corinthians from Macedonia. 2 Cor 8/9 possibly two
collection letters stitched together — a scholarly hypothesis, flagged as such.
Sources: text-internal + standard scholarship (Murphy-O'Connor, St. Paul's Corinth)

### Offender's identity (2 Cor 2:5-11) — used wk 42, 43
Verdict: genuinely debated — may be the 1 Cor 5 incest man, may be someone who
opposed Paul personally. Present as debated, never resolved.
Sources: standard commentaries (Furnish, Barnett)

## Lexicon & text

### hilastērion (Rom 3:25) — used wk 44
Verdict: "mercy seat" dominant (LXX's only concrete referent is the kapporet);
minority readings "propitiation"/"votive offering" remain debated.
Approved wording: "the same word the LXX uses for the mercy seat — the dominant
reading, though 'propitiation' and 'votive offering' are defended"
Sources: Bailey, Tyndale Bull. 51 (2000) · Tiwald, Tübingen

### opsōnia (Rom 6:23) — used wk 44
Verdict: confirmed — technical term for soldier's pay/rations (also Luke 3:14,
1 Cor 9:7).
Approved wording: "military wages — what a soldier is owed"
Sources: Hastings DNT "Wages" · standard lexicons (BDAG)

### Prov 22:8 LXX "cheerful giver" (2 Cor 9:7) — used wk 44
Verdict: confirmed — LXX alone carries ἄνδρα ἱλαρὸν καὶ δότην εὐλογεῖ ὁ θεός;
absent from the MT. Paul swaps ἀγαπᾷ for εὐλογεῖ.
Approved wording: "a line that exists only in the Greek OT, not the Hebrew —
LXX 'blesses,' Paul 'loves'"
Sources: OpenLXX Prov 22 · Expositor's Greek Testament ad loc.

### 1 Cor 15:3-5 creed — used wk 43
Verdict: pre-Pauline formula, consensus ≤5 yrs post-crucifixion — many put it at
Paul's ~AD 33-35 Jerusalem visit (Gal 1:18); Ehrman cautions "pre-Pauline" ≠ a
calendar date.
Approved wording: "most scholars date it to within ~5 years of the crucifixion
(many say ~3, at Paul's Jerusalem visit)"
Sources: MacGregor, JETS 49 · Pickup, JETS 56 · Wikipedia "1 Corinthians 15"

### "Baptized for the dead" (1 Cor 15:29) — used wk 43
Verdict: famously unresolved — Fee catalogues ~40 interpretations; other counts
run past 200. Two families: vicarious baptism for dead converts; baptism
motivated by dead loved ones.
Approved wording: "dozens of scholarly interpretations (Fee ~40, some counts
200+) — Paul's argument is logical, not an endorsement"
Sources: Sharp, BYU Studies/Scripture 6 · Oropeza via Logos

### Cicero & step-marriage (1 Cor 5:1) — used wk 42
Verdict: approximate parallel — Pro Cluentio (Sassia marrying her son-in-law
Melinus) is the standard cite; it's in-law incest not step-incest, and its legal
status then is debated.
Approved wording: "Cicero treats a comparable union (Pro Cluentio) as scandalous"
Sources: Pro Cluentio 5-6 · Unisa analysis (hdl.handle.net/10500/3538)

## Conventions

- Dates: `~AD NN` for approximate; always name the source when contested
  ("the Orosius date").
- Text-internal observations (quote chains, Deut 25:3 → 40 lashes, Ex 16:18 in
  2 Cor 8:15) need no external source — but state the reference.
- "Standard/scholarly" = uncontested reference-work claim; "consensus" =
  majority with noted minority; "debated" = live disagreement; "hypothesis" =
  proposed reconstruction.
